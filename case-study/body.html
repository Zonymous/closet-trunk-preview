The short answer

To automate SAP sales order entry with AI, let AI read the order and suggest the values, let fixed rules check them against SAP data, and have a person approve before a background service writes the contract to SAP. We built this for a packaging film manufacturer, and it is in production. The hard part was not reading documents. It was working out which film each customer means.

01

The problem: manual order entry in SAP.

Sales order automation sounds simple until you see how orders really arrive. Our client makes BOPP film, the thin plastic used in packaging and labels. Around 2,000 customers sit on its books, and about 70% of orders repeat something the customer has bought before. One month’s contract export gave us 326 contracts and 2,290 film lines.

Those orders rarely arrived in a neat format. Some were proper purchase orders. Others were a WhatsApp photo of a handwritten list, a phone call, or a two-line email. Customers wrote in their own shorthand: one who says “22 micron” means a specific grade called 22 TPT. Only an experienced sales manager knew what each customer meant.

The sales managers turned all of this into Excel sheets and emailed them to the desk. A desk operator then keyed every order into SAP by hand: 15 to 20 fields, a roll calculation, and then a second pass just for discounts. An officer checked the result by printing it and comparing it with the source.

03

The architecture: one rule shaped it.

The client’s concern about data and sign-off became our design principle: AI may read and suggest, fixed rules decide what is valid, and a person signs every write to SAP. Everything else follows from that, and it is the same way every workflow runs on our platform.

Order comes inCustomer portal, or the desk uploads a file or pastes an email
ReadingKnown spreadsheets go through plain rules. Photos and PDFs go to a language model
MatchingCustomer, film grade and terms are matched against SAP data and that customer’s history
Checks12 business checks, then a stricter check of the fields SAP needs
A person signsSales manager approval, desk review, and submit
SAPA background queue creates the contract, then reads back what SAP stored

Four choices did most of the work:

  • SAP never makes anyone wait. A submit joins a queue, the screen stays free, and a live status updates by itself.
  • Every order has one frozen reference. That is how we can always ask SAP whether it already has this order.
  • One database does the job of many tools. The queue lives in the same database as the orders, so there is less to run and less to break.
  • SAP stays the source of truth. Customers and films sync from SAP every night, and the app never overwrites them.

04

The interface: screens before spend.

We built a clickable prototype first and reviewed it with the people who would use it, so most arguments happened before any expensive build. We then shaped one product around four roles:

CustomerUpload an order or reorder in a few taps, then track it
Sales managerApprove orders on their own accounts and act for a customer
Desk teamReview, fix and submit to SAP, and raise change requests
AdminRelease contracts, approve changes and manage the team

Three ideas carried the design. A value the system guessed always says so, with its reason beside it. Any field can be edited where it sits, without opening a new form. And the staff copilot shows a plan card that does nothing until a person confirms it. A long screen-share review with the sales team later produced a 56-point list, which we worked through line by line.

The build: then we tried to break it.

The project grew to 42 screens, about 20 backend modules and around 1,650 automated backend checks. The test that taught us most was simple: put test orders through the real interface against a SAP test system, in a browser, the way a person would. We ran it three times, fixing what broke in between.

Run 2100 test orders

After 69 fixes: no refusals, and no duplicate through 63 minutes of SAP downtime.

In the scripted runs, an order took about 41 seconds from placed to released in SAP. That is system time only, with no human review in it.

06

Challenges of writing to SAP.

SAP’s answers could not be taken at face value

One order returned a server error, yet SAP had created the contract. A successful release came back as an error code. So we stopped trusting responses: before any retry the system asks SAP what it holds, and anything unclear stops for a person.

A customer’s 5,000 kg is not a quantity SAP can make

Film is made in whole rolls, so SAP offers the two nearest quantities and someone picks one. The new screen had to show the same choices before anything was sent. We rebuilt SAP’s roll calculation from the client’s own SAP logic, then compared it on 184 real order lines. The roll count matched on every one.

One purchase order, two contracts

Customers mix film families on one PO, but SAP needs some of them on separate contracts, and each contract needs its own reference. So the system splits the order, tells the person why before they place it, and numbers the references the way the client asked: PO, then PO/2.

Changing a contract already in production

A released contract is locked. Changing it means unreleasing it, applying the change and getting it released again, and SAP and our app must agree at every step. Early on, a failed check left one contract unreleased in SAP while our app said released. Now the checks run before SAP acts, and if SAP refuses a cancellation, the release is put back.

07

Security: built in and tested against the OWASP Top 10.

This system reads files from outside the company and writes to the ERP, so security was part of the design from day one.

Authentication and tokens

  • One-time codesThere are no passwords to steal. A code lasts 5 minutes, allows 5 attempts, and is stored only as a salted hash.
  • Access tokensSigned with RS256, valid for 15 minutes, checked on every request, and revocable at once.
  • Refresh tokensKept in an HttpOnly cookie that page scripts cannot read, stored only as a hash, and replaced on every use. If an old token comes back, the whole session is ended.

Encrypted data

Emails, phone numbers and names are encrypted field by field with AES-256, so a copy of the database does not reveal them. Exact-match search uses a separate blind index with its own key, so leaking one key does not expose both.

OWASP Top 10 testing

We ran penetration tests against all ten OWASP Top 10:2025 categories and the OWASP API Top 10, plus automated access checks for every role. We fixed what the tests found and re-tested each fix until it passed.

08

Keeping AI costs low.

Our first discovery was uncomfortable: our own spend tracker did not match the real bill. On one comparison the old figures ran about 2.7 times too high. So we fixed the measurement before we cut anything.

  • Don’t use AI where rules will do. Across 29 spreadsheets, plain rules matched the answer key 29 of 29 times, at no cost in about 5 milliseconds. Models got the same score for $0.28 to $0.86 in total and took 8 to 23 seconds.
  • Never pay twice. Uploading the same file again reuses the earlier reading for free. Small talk with the copilot uses no model at all.
  • Match the model to the job. A document read by AI cost under one US cent in our September logs. We benchmark candidate models on real documents and compare the score against the cost per file.
  • Hard limits. Each copilot turn has a step cap, and each user and the whole system have a daily spend cap.

09

How we got film grades right.

Reading a document is the easy part. The hard part is working out which film a customer means when the page says “25”. A wrong grade means the wrong film gets made, so this is where we spent the most time.

An early audit on 71 real customer documents was humbling. Only 70% of lines were either right or honestly left for a person to answer. The misses taught us more than the score did:

  • The customer’s past was missing.Most customers had no order history in the new app. We loaded their SAP contract history.
  • Part numbers looked like grades.A customer’s own item code was being read as the film. We now read the film from the thickness and the description.
  • Family words were too broad.“High COF glossy” fits several films. Now it narrows the list, and a person or that customer’s history picks from it.
  • Nothing was being remembered.Each confirmed choice now teaches the system what that customer’s words mean. One odd order can’t overturn a settled habit.

The result is a ladder. The system tries the surest answer first and stops when it has one. The AI only sees rows the rules could not settle, must choose from a short list of real candidates, and its answer is always marked for review.

  1. 1The exact film code is printed
  2. 2Thickness plus a written grade match
  3. 3The words point to exactly one film
  4. 4This customer’s own history, flagged
  5. 5AI picks from candidates, flagged
  6. 6Left blank, and a person answers

Where the 100% is

When the system filled a grade with no flag, it was right every time: 96 of 96 on this set, and 36 of 36 on a second set of 43 rows checked against SAP. The rest stay in front of a person: 20 flagged fills were right, 1 was wrong and 2 were left blank.

These are regression sets built partly from past bugs, so they guard against mistakes coming back. They do not predict results on new documents. Handwriting can still be misread, and a 49 can look like a 44, which is why a person reviews before anything reaches SAP.

10

The impact on the order desk.

The job moved from typing an order to checking one. To see why that matters, look at a single line. Keying it into SAP meant entering the film, width, thickness, core, outer diameter, treatment, packing and quantity, picking the nearest roll quantity, and adding the rate. That takes a few minutes, and an order is rarely one line.

Most contracts are small, but the big ones hurt. In one month’s export, the typical contract had 4 lines, and six of them had between 47 and 65.

What we would tell the next team

  • Let the AI say “I don’t know”. A blank a person fills costs seconds. A confident wrong grade costs a production run.
  • Test like a user. The worst bugs only appeared in full journeys, in a real browser, against the real ERP.
  • Measure before you optimise. That applies to accuracy and to the AI bill alike.

FAQ

Questions about AI sales order automation.

What is AI sales order automation?

It uses AI to read a customer’s order, in whatever format it arrives, and fill in the sales order for the ERP. In this project, fixed rules then check the order and a person approves it before it reaches SAP.

How long does manual SAP order entry take?

One line takes a few minutes, because it needs the film, width, thickness, core, outer diameter, treatment, packing, quantity, roll choice and rate. By our estimate from how the desk works, an order of 40 to 50 lines takes about an hour and a half.

Why is sales order automation hard?

Reading the document is the easy part. Customers use their own shorthand, so the system must learn what each one means. SAP also has strict rules on quantities, contracts and changes, and its responses cannot always be taken at face value.

Does the AI write directly to SAP?

No. The AI only reads and suggests. A background service writes to SAP through its own interfaces, and only after a person has approved the order.

Can it read handwritten orders and phone photos?

Yes. It reads phone photos, PDFs, spreadsheets and pasted emails. Handwriting is the hardest of these, so a person always reviews the order before it is placed.

How accurate is AI at matching film grades?

On our regression sets, every grade filled without a flag was right: 96 of 96 and 36 of 36. Grades it is unsure of are flagged or left blank for a person. These sets guard against old mistakes and do not predict results on new documents.

How do you secure an AI system connected to SAP?

Keep the AI away from SAP writes, use short-lived signed tokens with rotating refresh tokens, encrypt personal data, and check every role on the server. Then test against the OWASP Top 10, fix what you find, and re-test.

Is this running in production?

Yes. The platform is in production at the client. The test figures in this case study come from controlled runs against SAP test systems.
